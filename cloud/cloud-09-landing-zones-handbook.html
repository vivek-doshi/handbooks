Step 09 · Advanced · The secure, governed foundation every workload lands on

Landing zones and governance

A landing zone is the pre-built, governed environment where workloads are deployed. Getting it right once prevents years of inconsistency, security debt and cost sprawl.

HierarchyIdentityNetworkPolicy as codeSovereigntyAI guardrails

1. What a landing zone is

A landing zone is an opinionated, automated baseline: organisational hierarchy, identity, networking, security controls, logging, policy and cost management, plus a way to vend new accounts, subscriptions or projects that inherit all of it. It is a product operated by a platform team, not a one-off project.

2. Hierarchy and workload isolation

  • Mirror the hierarchy to ownership and risk, not to the org chart: platform and shared services, security and logging, sandbox, non-production, and production groups.
  • One account, subscription or project per workload and environment limits blast radius and simplifies cost allocation.
  • Apply guardrails at the group level (SCPs, management-group policy, organization policy) and let workload teams own everything inside them.
  • Provide an account vending pipeline: request, approve, provision with baseline, register in inventory.

3. Identity and access

  • One authoritative identity provider with SSO, MFA and lifecycle automation (joiner, mover, leaver); federate into every cloud.
  • Role-based access by job function, time-bound elevated access (just-in-time), and break-glass accounts that are monitored.
  • Workload identities: IAM roles, managed identities, service accounts with federation; ban long-lived keys.
  • Separate duties: those who can change guardrails are not those who build workloads.

4. Network foundations

  • Plan non-overlapping CIDR ranges across clouds and on-premises before deploying anything.
  • Hub-and-spoke or global transit (Transit Gateway, Virtual WAN, Network Connectivity Center) with centralised inspection and egress control.
  • Private connectivity to PaaS (PrivateLink, Private Link, Private Service Connect); default-deny; DNS strategy with private zones and conditional forwarding.
  • Zero-trust access to applications through identity-aware proxies rather than broad VPN access.

5. Security baselines

  • Organisation-wide audit logs to an immutable, separate security account.
  • Mandatory encryption, key management by tier, and blocked public storage by default.
  • Posture management (CSPM) and threat detection enabled automatically for every new account.
  • Vulnerability and configuration baselines aligned to CIS Benchmarks and your compliance framework.

6. Policy as code

Write guardrails as code and test them: SCPs and AWS Config, Azure Policy and Defender initiatives, Organization Policy and Policy Controller. Combine preventive controls (deny), detective controls (audit) and automated remediation. Use Open Policy Agent or Kyverno for cross-cloud and Kubernetes rules, and run the same checks in CI against Terraform plans so developers learn before deploy.

Control typeExample
PreventiveDeny public buckets, deny unapproved regions, require encryption
DetectiveFlag untagged or non-compliant resources

7. Sovereignty and residency

  • Classify data and map it to permitted regions and jurisdictions; enforce with region-restriction policies.
  • Use customer-managed or external keys for stronger control; understand each provider's sovereign cloud and partner offerings.
  • Consider regulations such as GDPR, the EU Data Act, NIS2 and DORA for financial entities when designing placement, exit plans and third-party risk.

8. AI and agent guardrails

  • Approve models and regions by policy; route model traffic through a central AI gateway for logging, quotas, redaction and safety filters.
  • Block uncontrolled use of public AI endpoints with data-loss prevention and egress policy.
  • Give agents managed identities, least-privilege tool access, spending limits and full audit trails.
  • Maintain an AI use-case register with risk tiering aligned to the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF.

9. Governance operating model

  • Define a platform team (builds and operates the zone), a cloud centre of excellence (standards and enablement) and workload owners (accountable for their accounts).
  • Publish a service catalogue, exception process and review cadence; measure compliance rate, time to provision, and drift.
  • Detect drift with scheduled plans and policy compliance reports; manage the zone through GitOps and versioned releases.
