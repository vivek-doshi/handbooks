Use Cases 1 to 3: Fraud, Recommendations and Forecasting

Three large classic-ML systems, each stressing a different part of the platform: streaming features and latency, embedding retrieval at huge scale, and massively parallel training.

Use case 1: Real-time fraud detection

ItemRequirement
Volume3 billion card transactions a month, 5,000 per second average, 25,000 peak
LatencyDecision under 100 ms end to end
LabelsChargebacks arrive 30 to 90 days late; fraud rate 0.1 percent
Data80 TB of history, 1.5 B accounts and devices
ConstraintsPCI data, explainability for declines, 99.99 percent availability
txn -> gateway -> feature service (online store: card/device/merchant aggregates) + stream features (Flink, 1m/1h/24h windows)
    -> rules engine (hard blocks) -> gradient-boosted model (served on CPU, p99 15 ms) -> decision + reason codes
    -> Kafka decision log -> lakehouse
Training: daily/weekly pipeline: label join (late), point-in-time features, time-based split, XGBoost/LightGBM,
          cost-weighted metrics (precision at fixed review capacity), calibration
Release: shadow 1 week -> canary 5 percent of traffic -> ramp; champion/challenger always running
Monitoring: feature drift, score distribution, approval rate, alert volume, delayed-label recall
  • Decisions: boosted trees on CPU beat deep models on latency and explainability; features matter more than model class. Use SHAP reason codes.
  • Skew control: one feature definition for batch backfill and Flink; point-in-time joins; freshness SLO of 5 seconds.
  • Delayed labels: train on matured labels, use proxy signals (analyst decisions) for fast feedback, and weight recent fraud patterns.
  • Capacity: 25k QPS at 500 QPS per replica is 50 replicas plus headroom; online store sized for hot keys; multi-region active-active.
  • Risks: adversarial adaptation, label bias (only reviewed cases have labels), outages causing fail-open. Mitigate with rules fallback, exploration traffic, and degraded mode.

Use case 2: Recommendations at 300 million users

ItemRequirement
Scale300 M users, 50 M items, 20 B interaction events a month
LatencyHome feed in 150 ms
GoalIncrease engagement and long-term retention, fresh items surface quickly
DataSeveral hundred TB of events; embeddings refreshed daily
Events (Kafka) -> lakehouse -> feature jobs + two-tower training (GPU, daily) -> user/item embeddings
Online:  candidate generation (ANN over item embeddings, multiple sources: collaborative, content, popularity, recent)
         -> ranking (deep ranker, 500 candidates -> 50, real-time features) -> re-ranking (diversity, business rules) -> feed
Release: offline recall@k/NDCG -> interleaving -> A/B on engagement and retention
Monitoring: coverage, diversity, popularity bias, freshness, feedback loops, latency per stage
  • Why multi-stage: you cannot score 50 M items per request; retrieve hundreds cheaply then rank accurately.
  • Embedding index: 50 M items x 128 dims int8 is about 6 GB per replica; shard for QPS, rebuild blue-green daily.
  • Training: distributed data-parallel on GPUs with sharded embedding tables; incremental fine-tuning between full runs; spot instances with checkpoints.
  • Evaluation: offline metrics mislead (position bias, feedback loops), so A/B and counterfactual evaluation are the arbiters.
  • Risks: filter bubbles, cold start, and exposure bias; mitigate with exploration and diversity constraints.

Use case 3: Demand forecasting for 200 million series

ItemRequirement
Scale200 M store-SKU series, daily history of 3 years, about 220 B observations
Output28-day forecast with prediction intervals, refreshed daily
GoalReduce stockouts and waste; planners can override
ConstraintsCost per forecast matters; many series are sparse
Lakehouse (partitioned by date/region) -> feature build (calendar, promos, price, weather, lags) in Spark
-> segment series: dense / intermittent / new (cold start)
-> Train: global model (LightGBM or deep global model) across all series + per-segment baselines;
          embarrassingly parallel by region on a Spark/Ray cluster; hyperparameter search on a sample
-> Backtest: rolling-origin cross-validation (never random splits), WAPE, bias, interval coverage
-> Batch inference nightly: write forecasts to a table, override layer for planners
Monitoring: forecast accuracy by segment vs naive baseline, bias, coverage; retrain weekly, full refresh monthly
  • Global model beats 200 M local models operationally and for sparse series; keep a seasonal-naive fallback.
  • Batch only: no online serving; cost is dominated by the feature build and the nightly scoring job, so partition and incremental processing matter.
  • Hierarchy: reconcile forecasts across SKU, store and region so totals agree.
  • Risks: promotions and shocks outside history, data gaps, planners distrusting a black box. Mitigate with explainable drivers, overrides logged as training signal, and accuracy dashboards.
