Step 10 · Intermediate · Trust but verify

Evaluation, Guardrails and Observability

Generative systems fail quietly. Evaluation, guardrails and observability are what turn a demo into a service you can change safely. Build them before scale, not after the first incident.

IntermediateEvalsGuardrails and tracing

Evaluation layers

Start smallThirty real examples with honest labels beat zero examples. Grow the set from production failures: every bug becomes a test case.

LLM-as-judge

A strong model scores outputs against a rubric. It scales human judgement but has biases: it prefers longer answers, its own style, and the first option shown.

JUDGE = """You grade an answer against the context.
Return JSON: {"faithful": true|false, "reason": "..."}.
faithful = every claim in the answer is supported by the context.

<context>{context}</context>
<answer>{answer}</answer>"""
  • Use a clear rubric with small integer scales or boolean checks, not vague 1 to 10 scores.
  • Randomise answer order in pairwise comparisons and use a different model family from the generator when possible.
  • Calibrate against 100 or so human-labelled examples and report agreement.
  • Prefer programmatic checks (exact match, tests, schema) wherever they exist.

Layer cheap deterministic checks first and model-based checks second, and measure the false-positive rate, since over-blocking harms the product as much as under-blocking.

Observability

  • Trace everything: one trace per request with spans for retrieval, reranking, each model call and tool call, including prompts, versions, token counts, latency and cost. OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions are the emerging standard.
  • Redact before storing: traces contain user data; apply PII masking and retention limits.
  • Dashboards and alerts: latency, error and refusal rates, cost per tenant, cache hit rate, retrieval-empty rate, guardrail triggers, user thumbs-up and down.
  • Feedback loop: sample failures into a review queue, label them, add them to the golden set, and use them for fine-tuning or retrieval fixes.
  • Drift: watch for changes in input mix, document corpus, provider model updates and cost.

Governance and risk

  • Use the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications as a threat checklist (injection, sensitive information disclosure, supply chain, excessive agency, and more).
  • Map to frameworks your organisation follows: NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, and regional rules such as the EU AI Act where relevant. Document intended use, data sources, evaluation results and human oversight.
  • Keep model and prompt change logs, access reviews for tools and data, and an incident process for harmful outputs.
  • Review vendor terms: training on your data, retention, regional processing and sub-processors.

Practice

Practice
  1. Create a 30-question golden set for your RAG app and run it in CI.
  2. Add a groundedness judge and measure agreement with your own labels on 20 answers.
  3. Add tracing and find your slowest span.
