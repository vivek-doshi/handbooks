Step 14 · Advanced · Large-scale RAG and tuned models

Use Cases 1 and 2: Knowledge Assistant and Support at Scale

Two systems that look similar and need opposite answers to the fine-tuning question. The knowledge assistant is a retrieval problem at huge data scale; the support platform is a volume and behaviour problem where a tuned small model pays for itself.

Advanced1.5B chunks30M conversations a month

How to read a case study

Each case follows the same method: requirements and scale, then the decision ladder from step 12, architecture, data pipeline, serving capacity, evaluation, security and cost, and finally risks.

About the numbersAll figures are illustrative planning numbers to show the method. Replace them with measured token counts, current prices and benchmark results for your own models and hardware.

Use case 1: Enterprise knowledge assistant

ItemRequirement
Users200,000 employees, 50,000 daily active users, around 10 questions a day each
Corpus120 million documents across SharePoint, Confluence, Google Drive, Slack, ticketing and wikis; about 1.5 billion chunks; 40 percent changes weekly
Traffic500,000 questions a day; peak about 40 requests a second
QualityAnswer with citations; refuse when evidence is missing; respect each user's document permissions exactly
LatencyFirst token under 2 seconds; full answer under 10 seconds
ConstraintsData stays in region; full audit trail; no training of vendor models on company data

UC1 decisions

QuestionDecisionReason
Fine-tune the generator?No, use a frontier or strong open model with a strict grounded-answer promptThe problem is knowledge and freshness, not behaviour
Fine-tune retrieval?Yes, later: embedder and reranker tuned on click and citation logsCompany vocabulary and acronyms; the highest-value tuning here
RAG strategy?Hybrid (dense + BM25), ACL pre-filtering, cross-encoder rerank, parent-child chunks, query rewriting; agentic retrieval only for complex queriesQuality from fundamentals; agents only where needed
Contextual chunk enrichment?Selectively, for high-value sources1.5B LLM calls would be prohibitive
Graph?Entity graph for people, teams and projects, used by a router for relationship questionsCheap, targeted

UC1 architecture

Sources (SharePoint, Confluence, Drive, Slack, tickets)
   -> Connectors (webhooks + incremental crawl + ACL sync) -> Kafka
   -> Parse (layout-aware) -> dedupe (MinHash) -> chunk (structure-aware) -> embed (GPU workers)
   -> Index: sharded vector engine + BM25 + metadata (acl_groups, source, updated_at)

Query path:
 user -> gateway (SSO) -> conversation rewrite -> router
      -> retrieval: ACL pre-filter -> dense top 100 + BM25 top 100 -> RRF -> rerank top 8
      -> prompt (cache-friendly prefix) -> LLM -> citations check -> answer
 side: trace + eval sampling, feedback (thumbs, clicks) -> training data for embedder and reranker
  • Permissions are the hard part. Sync ACLs continuously. Store group ids on chunks, resolve the caller's groups at query time, and filter inside the index. A permissions change must reach the index within minutes, and deleted documents must disappear.
  • Index memory: 1.5B vectors at 1024 dimensions in float32 is 6 TB. Use Matryoshka truncation to 512 dimensions and int8 (about 770 GB) or binary (about 96 GB) for the first pass held in RAM across a sharded cluster, then rescore the top few hundred with full vectors on SSD.
  • Backfill: about 600 billion tokens to embed. At 1 million tokens per second across a GPU fleet that is about a week; scale workers 5 to 10 times for the initial load and prioritise high-traffic sources first.
  • Freshness: event-driven incremental updates, with a nightly reconciliation crawl to catch missed events.
  • Tenancy and blast radius: partition indexes by region and business unit to keep shards small and failures contained.

UC1 capacity and cost

AreaEstimate
LLM calls40 req/s peak, about 6,000 input tokens (8 chunks plus prompt), 300 output tokens; prefix caching cuts repeated system and policy tokens
RetrievalAbout 40 queries a second, each fanned out across shards, target p95 under 150 ms for retrieval plus rerank
RerankTop 100 candidates per query is about 4,000 pairs a second; one or two GPU replicas, or a managed reranker
Index nodesAround 12 to 24 memory-optimised nodes with replicas, depending on quantisation choice
Largest cost driversLLM tokens and index memory; control with model routing, prompt trimming and binary or int8 vectors
  • Evaluate: 500-question golden set stratified by source and department, recall@10, faithfulness, permission-leak tests (must be zero), and online thumbs-up rate.
  • Risks: permission leaks, stale or conflicting documents, prompt injection through shared documents, and cost growth. Mitigate with ACL tests in CI, freshness and authority ranking, injection defences, and budgets.

Use case 2: Customer support platform

ItemRequirement
Volume30 million conversations a month, average 6 turns, 40 languages
Knowledge3 million knowledge-base articles and 200 million historical resolved tickets
GoalsDeflect 35 percent of conversations, draft replies for human agents on the rest, keep resolution quality at or above human baseline
ActionsOrder lookup, address change, refund up to a limit with approval, escalation to humans
LatencyUnder 3 seconds to first token
ConstraintsTone and policy compliance, regulated refund rules, PII protection, multi-region

UC2 decisions

QuestionDecisionReason
Which model serves most traffic?Fine-tuned 8B open model (SFT on about 500k resolved agent replies, then DPO on satisfaction-ranked pairs), with a frontier model for the long tail180 million model calls a month make per-token pricing of a large model the dominant cost
Why fine-tune?Brand tone, policy-faithful format, tool-call reliability, 40 languages, shorter promptsBehaviour at volume is a fine-tuning problem
Facts?RAG over KB articles and similar resolved ticketsPolicies and products change weekly; never train facts in
Routing?Fine-tuned small intent classifier: FAQ, account action, complex or sensitive, escalateCheap first hop; sensitive topics go to humans or the strongest model
Embedder and reranker?Fine-tune on ticket to resolving-article pairsStrong supervised signal already exists in the data
Actions?Tools with schemas; refund over the limit requires human approval; idempotent callsLeast privilege and auditability

UC2 architecture

Channels (chat, email, WhatsApp, voice->ASR) -> gateway -> conversation service (state, memory)
   -> PII redaction -> intent router (small tuned classifier)
        |-- FAQ / how-to : RAG (KB hybrid + resolved-ticket retrieval) -> tuned 8B generator
        |-- account action: tool-calling agent (order, address, refund) -> policy checker -> tools
        |-- complex / sensitive / low confidence: frontier model or human handoff
   -> guardrails (policy, tone, PII, groundedness) -> reply + citations
 Human agent assist: same retrieval + draft reply, agent approves; outcomes feed training data

Training loop: transcripts -> redact -> quality filter (CSAT, resolution, no escalation)
   -> SFT + DPO datasets -> train (QLoRA or full) -> eval + safety regression -> registry
   -> canary 5 percent -> rollout; retrain quarterly or on policy change

UC2 capacity and economics

180M calls / month -> ~70 req/s average, ~250 req/s peak
Per call: ~1,200 input tokens (with retrieved context), ~150 output tokens
Peak prefill ~300k tokens/s, peak decode ~37k tokens/s
8B model, FP8, continuous batching on H100-class GPUs: benchmark ~3k to 5k decode tokens/s/GPU
=> roughly 10 to 16 GPUs for decode at peak, plus prefill headroom; plan ~24 GPUs across 2 regions (N+1)
Frontier fallback for ~8 percent of calls = ~14M calls/month -> budgeted with a hard cap
Savings versus a frontier-only design: order of 5x to 10x per conversation (verify with your prices)
  • Evaluate: golden set of 1,000 real conversations by intent and language; policy-compliance judge; groundedness; tool-call accuracy; CSAT; deflection and re-contact rate.
  • Safeguards: block refunds and account changes above limits without approval; run red-team suites for jailbreaks and data requests; keep human handoff always one click away.
  • Risks: policy drift (stale training), multilingual quality gaps, over-automation. Mitigate with RAG for policy facts, per-language evals, and phased rollout by intent.
